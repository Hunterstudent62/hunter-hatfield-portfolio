SPECIES BACKGROUND
1. Range
2. Habitat
3. What is it?
4. Conservation status
1. Cuba, Isla de la Juventud, and surrounding Cuban cays/islets
2. Rocky coastal areas, limestone refuges, coastal scrub, mangroves, 
and sandy shore vegetation
3. Large diurnal iguanian lizard; a West Indian rock iguana in genus 
Cyclura; opportunistic herbivore
4. IUCN/FWS: Vulnerable / federally threatened; still one of the more 
common rock iguanas

SANDWICH BACKGROUND
1. ID / body condition
2. Current husbandry
3. Diet behavior
4. Other Observations
1. 1.0 Cuban iguana; ~9 lb; estimated 10–15 years old; body 
condition appears good.
2. Fed M/W/F; enclosure is misted and adjusted for temperature; 
most basking occurs before noon.
3. Currently consumes ~99%; prefers fruits primarily., encouraged to 
forage
4. Nasal salt-gland excretion common in iguanas.


===== PAGE 4 =====

WILD DIET ANAL YSIS
• Generalist/opportunistic herbivore: Mainly leaves, fruits, 
flowers, grasses, and locally available vegetation.
• Some natural plant items: Cocoplum, saltwort, buttonwood, 
seagrass, grasses, and Metopium toxiferum fruit.
• Seasonal fruit consumption: Cayo Sijú June scat: 96.7% 
contained Chrysobalanus icaco fruit. In June, fruit was the 
single most frequent item.
• Documented animal matter: Palomo scat: 45% dragonfly 
remains, 5% crab parts; Gerber documented forceful carrion 
feeding on a dove nestling.
• Breakdown: Plants dominate overall, fruit may become 
seasonally dominant, and animal matter is minor but 
diverse/opportunistic.

===== PAGE 8 =====

WHAT WE CAN CONCLUDE
1. Compared with captive diets
2. Compared with wild diets
3. Main limitation
4. Overall conclusion
1. ZooTampa and Lincoln are very similar; Sandwich is slightly lower in 
protein than Lincoln, while Miami/Jamaican is the major outlier because 
of Mazuri and higher energy/fiber.
2. Wild sources suggest more seasonal fruit and occasional animal matter 
than the current base diet; Sandwich’s browse and foraging presentation 
still make the diet more naturalistic.
3. ZDN is useful for side-by-side trends, but not for proving requirements 
without a Cuban iguana profile; browse, supplements, and keeper 
additions are not fully captured.
4. Supplements are more varied at ZooTampa, but appear less frequent/less 
standardized than Lincoln’s daily calcium and Miami’s ReptiCalcium w/D3 
each feed day.

===== PAGE 9 =====

SUGGESTIONS/
POSSIBLE DIETARY
CHANGES
• Keep base diet mostly intact; no major regular 
item looks clearly inappropriate.
• Increase seasonal fruit variety and possibly 
quantity in a controlled way, since wild Cuban 
iguanas may eat fruit heavily during some 
seasons. (can outnumber leaf material in some 
wild samples) Wild fruits are less sugar dense 
than grocery counterparts so caution advised.
• Clarify/standardize supplement rotation and 
document the reason for frequency.
• Consider rare animal-based enrichment only 
with veterinary approval: insects/hornworms 
first; even crayfish/crab or chick could be rare 
enrichment based on wild diet data.
• Do not add Mazuri/Complete feeds unless ZDN 
or staff review suggests a specific nutrient gap.
